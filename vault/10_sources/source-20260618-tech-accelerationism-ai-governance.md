# 科技加速主义与人工智能治理

## Why Save

This article is useful for Prajnavex because it gives a governance-theory frame for current AI policy competition. Its main value is not a list of regulations, but a conceptual move: when substantive ethical consensus is hard, AI governance may need to shift toward safety systems, procedural justification, verifiable risk controls, and structured human-machine dialogue.

## Source Assessment

The article argues that 2025 marked a global policy turn toward technology accelerationism. The United States, China, Japan, South Korea, and even parts of Europe are described as moving away from strict AI restraint and toward development-first policy. This makes ethics-centered AI governance harder to sustain as the dominant paradigm.

The author does not reject ethics. Instead, he argues that ethics is difficult to operationalize globally because values differ across civilizations, institutions, and policy communities. The proposed alternative is a "technology-procedure" paradigm: use technical safety systems to handle AI safety risks, then use fair procedures, risk communication, and argumentative dialogue to generate legitimacy and shared norms.

China's AI governance proposal is interpreted as an example of this shift. The article highlights AI safety systems, international capacity-building, technical standards, open platforms, and the proposed World AI Cooperation Organization as ways to create a global minimum consensus without first resolving all ethical disagreements.

## Useful Ideas

- AI governance has two hard problems: integration of competing norm systems and execution of weak or fragmented standards.
- Technology accelerationism changes the political economy of AI regulation by making development-first policy more attractive to states and firms.
- Ethics-centered AI governance is flexible but hard to define, hard to compare across civilizations, and hard to embed in technical reasoning.
- Separating AI safety from AI ethics can create a broader consensus surface.
- A "technology-procedure" paradigm treats procedure as the bridge between technical control and human values.
- Procedural justice can function as a generator of norms rather than a mere implementation detail.
- China and the United States both move beyond pure ethics-centered governance, but they differ in geopolitical frame: public-good multilateralism versus strategic leadership and ecosystem dominance.
- Generative AI predicts probabilities rather than producing meaning, so humanistic value formation remains a human responsibility.
